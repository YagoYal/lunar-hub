A crescent moon's sunlit edges frame the faintly illuminated lunar night side in this photograph captured on April 19. The two-day-old Moon was positioned near perigee while also appearing close to the Pleiades Star Cluster. As moonlight was dimmed by clouds, the Pleiades stars became visible beneath the bright crescent through a faint lunar corona.

The dark lunar surface glows through earthshine — sunlight reflected from Earth itself. This ashen glow, termed "the old moon in the young moon's arms," appears particularly bright during northern hemisphere spring. The Moon's orbital path brings it near the Pleiades monthly, with these conjunctions most easily observed when the Moon exhibits a crescent phase.
