The Mare Orientale, meaning "Eastern Sea," ranks among the Moon's most visually striking large-scale formations. As the youngest of the large lunar impact basins, it remains challenging to observe from Earth due to its location along the lunar edge. This telescopic photograph, captured on July 7, 2026 under favorable viewing geometry, reveals the feature as extremely foreshortened along the Moon's western limb.

Created over 3 billion years ago by an asteroid impact spanning nearly 1000 kilometers, the basin displays concentric circular patterns representing ripples within the lunar crust. These features appear more distinctly in direct orbital images of the region.

The nomenclature paradox — why an "Eastern" sea appears on the Moon's western edge — stems from a 1961 convention reversal that changed east and west on lunar maps.
