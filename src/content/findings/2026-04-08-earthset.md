On April 6, 2026, astronaut Victor Glover transmitted a message from lunar orbit: "And to all of you down there on Earth... we love you, from the Moon." As the Artemis II crew conducted their seven-hour lunar flyby, they observed Earth setting below the Moon's horizon and characterized previously unseen regions of the lunar far side.

The expedition documented lunar surface features including crater peaks, floors, terraces, and rings — geological records that help scientists reconstruct the Solar System's impact history. Notable observations included the well-preserved Orientale basin and the identification of two previously unknown craters. The far side's puzzlingly diminished volcanic activity compared to the near side remains an important scientific puzzle.
