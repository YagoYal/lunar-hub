The photograph captures the Full Moon rising as the Sun set from Teide National Park in the Canary Islands. The moon appears orange-tinted and slightly flattened. What makes this image special is the timing: the Full Moon is truly at its full phase when it is Pi radians (aka 180 degrees) from the Sun in ecliptic longitude.

The image also shows the triangular shadow of Teide volcano extending upward into Earth's atmosphere, visible from about 3,500 meters elevation. The white telescope domes of Teide Observatory are visible along the distant ridge.

The photograph was taken on a date that marks a cosmic coincidence: it was both Pi Day and the moment when the moon is Pi radians from the Sun. This timing aligns with approximately three minutes before the midpoint of a total lunar eclipse occurring that same day.
