NASA's WB-57F aircraft pursued the Moon's shadow off Iceland's coast on August 12, flying at 50,000 feet along the eclipse's totality path. The high-altitude research plane carried specialized cameras that documented the eclipse from above atmospheric interference. This cockpit image captures the solar corona emerging at the beginning of totality, with the sky darkened by the Moon's shadow. Beyond the shadow's reach, the distant horizon glows brightly. Venus appears left of center, while Jupiter and Mercury are barely visible to the right of the eclipsed Sun.
